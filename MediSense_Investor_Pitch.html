The problem

Diagnosis is the bottleneck — and it doesn't scale with headcount.

Specialist supply grows linearly. Demand, complexity, and documentation burden do not. The result is the same everywhere: long queues, uneven quality by geography, and diagnostic error concentrated in exactly the cases that matter most.

Access is unevenly distributed

Diagnostic quality depends heavily on which hospital tier a patient reaches — a gap national health systems are now explicitly funding technology to close.

Anchoring is structural

Under time pressure clinicians converge early on a leading hypothesis. Rare and do-not-miss conditions are the systematic casualty.

Prescribing risk is manual

Interaction, allergy, dosing and duplicate-therapy screening still depends on individual vigilance at the point of writing.

Why now

Clinical AI just crossed from pilot budget to paid infrastructure.

For a decade, diagnostic AI was bought out of innovation budgets and measured in pilots. Three things changed at once — and they changed the business model, not just the technology.

Reimbursed
A major national payer now pays for AI-assisted diagnosis as a billable clinical item — turning adoption from a cost centre into revenue for the hospital.
Regulated

Governed
Human-in-the-loop sign-off is becoming a codified legal standard rather than a design preference — which favours explainable, auditable systems over black boxes.

The product

Describe the presentation. Get a ranked differential, with its reasoning.

MediSense cross-references symptoms, history and prescriptions against a clinical knowledge base and a database of real diagnostic episodes — and returns something a clinician can interrogate, not just accept.

Ranked differential

Calibrated probabilities across a broad hypothesis set — not a single black-box answer.

Evidence drawer

Supporting and contradicting signals, similar historical cases, and the next-best test for each hypothesis.

Prescription safety

Every proposed treatment screened live for interactions, allergies, dosing and duplicate therapy.

Acuity triage

Live auto-ranked patient queue with escalation alerts, scored by criticality and urgency.

Anti-anchoring by design

Do-not-miss conditions are surfaced even at low probability. The system is built to widen the differential, not narrow it prematurely.

Standards-based integration

HL7 FHIR resources with an adapter layer for existing hospital systems; role-scoped access enforced at the API boundary.

How it compounds

Every closed episode is a training signal.

MediSense learns from complete diagnostic episodes — not from labels alone. The outcome closes the loop, and every clinician override is itself supervision.

01

Presentation

Symptoms, history and context captured in plain language at the point of care.

02

Differential

Ranked hypotheses with calibrated probability and attached evidence.

03

Clinician decision

Accept, adjust or override — with the reasoning visible. Sign-off is required.

04

Treatment

Prescription proposed and screened; hard safety rules override statistics.

05

Outcome

Recovery result labels the episode and feeds the next patient's differential.

Moat logic: outcome-labelled, override-annotated episodes inside a deployed hospital workflow are the asset that is hard to buy and hard to copy. Model weights are not the moat; the episode corpus and the workflow position are.
Safety & governance

Assist, never replace — enforced in architecture, not in policy language.

The binding national safety standard is the Human-Machine Double Signature: the machine may suggest, the licensed clinician must sign. MediSense is built around that constraint rather than retrofitted to it.

  • Cannot act autonomously on a patient. Human-in-the-loop is structural.
  • Hard clinical rules win. Allergies and contraindications override any statistical suggestion.
  • Fail-safe defaults. On low confidence or missing data the system asks rather than guesses.
  • Graceful degradation. A degraded component yields a labelled, reduced answer; safety blocks always hold.
  • Tamper-evident audit trail. Every view, suggestion, override and sign-off recorded immutably and traceable to a model version.

Compliance posture

  • Encryption in transit and at rest; data minimization; de-identification for learning; configurable data residency.
  • Algorithms must pass national data security certification before deployment.
  • Patient biometric data cannot be called, stored or used without authorization — the most consistently repeated rule across all sources.
  • Non-registered clinical research follows STROBE reporting with prior ethics approval.
Regulatory-landscape context: only 3.6% of FDA-approved AI devices historically reported racial demographic data in validation — the reason 2025/26 guidance now mandates dataset diversity. A US-context statistic, used here as landscape, not as a China-market claim.

Regulatory strategy

Dual track: ship on Class II, build the moat on Class III.

The classification boundary is the strategy. Class II reaches the market and starts the episode flywheel. Class III is the durable barrier — and the reason the moat cannot be crossed quickly by a well-funded fast follower.

Class II

Data processing and measurement; clinical reference only, not decision support. Faster to market, starts data accumulation.

Class III

Clinical decision support — lesion identification and treatment planning. The classification that governs a true diagnostic product, and the real barrier to entry.

The approval window

  • Current NMPA cycle: 4–8 months.
  • Special Approval Channel cuts review time by ~83 days.
  • AI diagnostic software is generally Class III (high risk).

Business model

Five payers, one product.

Reimbursement inclusion does not create a single revenue line — it creates five, each with a different buying logic and cycle. Concentration risk falls as the payer mix broadens.

1 · Insurance

The foundational engine. Drives baseline diagnostic volume; 70–85% of the listed fee for a Category B item.

2 · Hospital

Direct procurement, justified on departmental efficiency and compliance mandate rather than on reimbursement alone.

3 · Pharma

Outcome-based models and clinical trial support — the real-world-evidence wedge. Highest margin, longest cycle.

4 · Commercial insurance

Supplemental coverage for high-end diagnostic suites beyond the state catalog.

5 · Individual

The residual 15–30% self-pay share of the listed price.
